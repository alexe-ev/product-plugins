Key Findings

  • Overall delta +0.143 is modest. The skill provides measurable but limited improvement over baseline. Opus 4.6 is already strong at hypothesis validation without a skill.
  • E3 is completely non-discriminating (Δ = 0.000). Well-formed hypotheses are correctly identified by both configs. This is a ceiling effect — the eval confirms both handle the easy case, but provides no useful signal for benchmarking.
  • Primary skill value: verdict discipline. The strongest discriminators are verdict-related (correct "NOT ready" classification, proper "weak" labeling, no premature experiment design). Without the skill, the model sometimes produces overly permissive verdicts.
  • E2 baseline variance is high (stddev=0.218). One baseline run perfectly identified all issues (7/7), while another scored only 4/7. The skill eliminates this variance entirely (stddev=0.000).
  • Scope discipline is the skill's strongest contribution. The "no experiment design elements" assertion (E1) shows 3/3 vs 0/3 — without the skill, the model always adds sample sizes, durations, and traffic splits even for flawed hypotheses.
  • 76.2% of assertions are non-discriminating. Most checks (identifying mixed changes, providing improved versions, listing missing info) are things the model does well without the skill.

Recommendations for Iteration 2

  • Replace E3 entirely. A non-discriminating eval wastes 6 runs. Consider a borderline case — e.g., a hypothesis that's close to valid but has a subtle flaw (like an overly ambitious uplift target with no evidence, or a mechanism that sounds plausible but has known confounds).
  • Replace non-discriminating assertions. In E1 and E2, assertions like "identifies mixed changes", "provides improved version", and "lists missing info" all pass 3/3 in both configs. Replace with harder checks — e.g., "output correctly classifies the hypothesis into the skill's 4-tier verdict system" or "output does not provide statistical power calculations for a weak hypothesis".
  • Consider adding an eval for "valid for exploration" verdict. The skill defines 4 verdict tiers but we only test 2 (weak and valid for experiment). A hypothesis that's interesting but not experiment-ready would test the nuanced middle ground.
  • Strengthen the skill's verdict calibration. The main discriminating assertions relate to verdict discipline. The SKILL.md could benefit from stronger language about when NOT to include experiment design elements, and more explicit decision criteria for each verdict tier.
