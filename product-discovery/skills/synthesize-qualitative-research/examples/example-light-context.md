## Input

Product: Finly — a B2C mobile app for personal finance.

Context: "We did about 5 interviews with users who churned. People said they stopped using the app after a while. A couple of them said they liked the savings tracker. We didn't record the sessions so we're working from memory."

## Expected output

Research questions (inferred): Why do users stop using Finly after initial sign-up? What features, if any, feel valuable?

Key themes (provisional — memory-based, not verified):
- Disengagement occurs at some point after initial use — specific trigger or timing not confirmed from recall
- Savings tracker viewed positively by at least some users — what made it valuable is not documented

Top insights (provisional — low reliability, treat as hypotheses only):
1. Users disengage from Finly after initial use, but the root cause of disengagement is not established from available data.
2. The savings tracker may be a retention-relevant feature — insufficient data to confirm the mechanism or its effect size.

Outliers: Unknown — without notes, outlying responses cannot be identified or separated from the main pattern.

Research gaps: Almost everything that would be needed to act on this:
- No transcripts or structured notes; recall bias is likely and unquantifiable
- Unable to distinguish response patterns between participants
- Trigger moment and timing of disengagement not captured
- Reason savings tracker was valued (if it was) not documented
- No contrast with retained users

Confidence level: Thin-data — this synthesis is directional only. No product decisions should be made based on this alone.

Recommended next step: Conduct 5–8 additional structured interviews with notes or recording. Use the two provisional themes above as starting hypotheses to probe, not as findings to act on. If re-interviews are not possible, supplement with support ticket analysis or exit survey data from the same cohort.
