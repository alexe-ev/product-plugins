## Input

Product: Finly — a B2C mobile app for personal finance.

Context: "Our interviews suggested users want to feel in control, and some people mentioned being anxious about money. We had about 5 interviews but didn't take detailed notes — we're working from memory."

## Expected output

Insight 1 (provisional): Users may engage with Finly primarily to reduce financial anxiety rather than to perform precise expense tracking.
- Supporting evidence: Team recall of "wanting to feel in control" and "anxiety about money" language across approximately 5 interviews.
- Evidence quality: Single data point — memory-based recall with no structured notes. Cannot verify frequency, exact wording, or whether this was consistent or incidental.
- Confidence: Low.

Insight 2 (provisional): "Feeling in control" may mean different things to different users — a meaningful distinction may exist between users who seek proactive control (planning) and users who seek reactive relief (checking after something feels wrong).
- Supporting evidence: Implied variation in the team's recall of "in control" language, but not confirmed from notes.
- Evidence quality: Single data point — inferred, not directly observed.
- Confidence: Low.

Contradictory or complicating data: Unknown — without notes, contradictions cannot be identified.

What changes relative to prior assumptions: Unknown — without documented prior assumptions, cannot compare.

Gaps: Almost everything that would be needed to act on these insights:
- No transcripts or structured notes — recall bias is likely and unquantifiable
- Frequency of themes cannot be assessed
- No distinction between participants — cannot tell if this came from one person or all five
- Cannot separate observations (what users said) from interpretations (what the team inferred)

Note: These are candidate insights only — they should be treated as hypotheses to validate in the next research round, not findings to act on.

Recommended next step: Re-run 5–8 interviews with structured note-taking. Use these provisional insights as probing themes — ask specifically about what "control" means behaviourally and what triggers financial anxiety — rather than treating them as confirmed.
