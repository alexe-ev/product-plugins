# Experiment Hypothesis: Button Color and Conversion

## Input (Original Task)
"Конверсия падает, надо что-то делать. Думаем поменять цвет кнопки. Это должно поднять конверсию на 30%."

Translation: "Conversion is falling, we need to do something. We're thinking about changing the button color. This should raise conversion by 30%."

---

## Context Assessment
This is a **context-poor** input. While a proposed intervention (button color change) and a desired outcome direction (increase conversion) are mentioned, critical information is missing, and a quantitative impact claim is unsupported.

---

## Discovery Summary

### What is known:
- There is a conversion decline (observed problem)
- A button color change is proposed as the intervention
- The team expects this to improve conversion

### What is unclear or missing:
- **Which product area?** What is the button used for? (CTA on landing page, sign-up form, checkout, etc.?)
- **Which users or segment?** All users? New users? Returning users? Desktop or mobile? Geographic or market segment?
- **What type of conversion?** Sign-up? Purchase? Account activation? Feature adoption?
- **What is the current conversion rate?** Baseline metric needed.
- **Why button color specifically?** What is the current color? What evidence suggests color is the issue?
- **Funnel context?** What stage of the funnel is this button in?

### Evidence basis for 30% uplift claim:
The input asserts "should raise conversion by 30%" with no cited evidence, prior tests, industry benchmarks, or documented rationale.

---

## Provisional Hypothesis

**If we change the button color for [unspecified user segment],
we expect [conversion metric type] to increase,
because button color may affect visual prominence and user engagement.**

---

## Extracted Information

**Target segment:**
Unconfirmed. Likely all users or all visitors to a specific page, but segment clarity is needed.

**Likely primary metric:**
Conversion rate (type unspecified). Could be: sign-up rate, purchase completion rate, feature activation rate, or other step in a funnel.

**Expected direction:**
Increase

**Why this might work:**
Button color can influence visual hierarchy, contrast, and user attention. A more prominent or psychologically aligned color might encourage more clicks and completions.

**Expected impact hypothesis:**
**Not adopted.** The assertion of 30% uplift is unsupported and should not be carried forward until:
- Prior test results show button color changes drive uplift in this product
- Industry benchmarks or research specifically apply to this button type and context
- A documented rationale is provided by the team

**Flagged issue:** Instruction #10 (expected impact hypothesis) directs to "flag it under Missing information as: 'impact assumption needs validation — no evidence basis provided.'" This applies here.

---

## Missing Information (Critical)

Before moving to hypothesis validation or experiment design, confirm:

1. **Product area:** Which page, feature, or funnel does this button belong to?
2. **Specific button identity:** What is the button's current color? What CTA text does it have?
3. **Target user segment:** All users, new users, logged-in users, paid users, or a specific cohort?
4. **Conversion metric definition:** Exactly which step or action defines success?
5. **Current baseline:** What is the current conversion rate for this metric?
6. **Evidence or rationale for button color:** Why is color suspected as a bottleneck? Any user research, heatmap data, or clickthrough patterns?
7. **Why 30% specifically?** What is the source of this number? Is it a team guess, a past result in another product, or an industry benchmark?

---

## Confidence Level of Framing

**Provisional**

The proposed change (button color) is stated, but:
- The product area, target segment, and metric type are not confirmed
- The button's role in the funnel is unclear
- The causal mechanism (why color would move this metric) is not explained
- The 30% impact claim lacks any evidence foundation

This hypothesis is a starting structure, not a validated one. It is ready to move to discovery/clarification, not yet to validation or experiment design.

---

## Suggested Next Steps

1. **Clarify the product context:** Which product area, page, or funnel stage?
2. **Define the metric:** What exactly is "conversion" in this context?
3. **Understand the problem:** Is low color contrast the bottleneck, or is button color a guess?
4. **Gather evidence:** Are there user research, analytics, or design feedback that suggest button color is an issue?
5. **Revisit the impact assumption:** Remove or replace the 30% claim with a directional hypothesis or a benchmarked estimate.
