## Input

Product: Finly — a B2C mobile app for personal finance targeting young adults aged 22–32.

Available research: 8 user interviews completed with new users. Key themes from notes:
- Users frequently mentioned payday as a moment of financial anxiety mixed with optimism ("new month, fresh start")
- Several described wanting to feel "on top of things" without it becoming an obsession
- Competing alternatives mentioned: spreadsheets ("too much effort to maintain"), bank apps ("they just show me what I spent, not what to do"), and ignoring finances entirely ("default when anxiety wins")
- Churned users described the app as making them feel judged; retained users described it as giving them a sense of control

## Expected output

Job statement: "When I receive my paycheck and start thinking about the month ahead, I want to quickly feel that my money is under control, so I can stop worrying and get on with the rest of my life."

Functional dimension: Allocate incoming money to spending categories, check whether current spend is on track, confirm savings are progressing. The task is a fast check-in, not a detailed financial review.

Emotional dimension: Feel calm and in control of finances without obsessing over every transaction. Avoid the anxiety that comes from financial uncertainty. Feel responsible and capable, not restricted or judged.

Social dimension: Be the kind of person who "has their finances sorted" — not a reckless spender, but also not someone who tracks every penny obsessively. The perceived identity value is quiet competence.

Struggling moment: Payday — the moment when money arrives and users simultaneously feel hopeful (new month, fresh start) and anxious (will I manage it this time?). Secondary struggling moment: an unexpected expense that disrupts a plan and creates a need to recalibrate.

Competing solutions for the same job:
- Bank app: Official but stressful — shows what happened, not what to do next; feels like receiving a verdict
- Spreadsheet: Powerful but abandoned — too much effort to set up and maintain; doesn't fit into daily life
- Ignoring finances entirely: The default when anxiety exceeds the perceived value of engaging

Design implications: The payday moment is the natural activation trigger — Finly should offer a fast, low-effort "check-in" ritual at the start of each pay period that feels forward-looking (what's the plan?) rather than backward-looking (what went wrong?). The emotional job — reducing anxiety without creating new stress — should shape the tone and structure of every interaction.

Evidence quality: Interview-based — consistent pattern across 8 interviews; functional and emotional jobs well-supported; social job inferred from language but not explicitly stated by users.

Open questions:
- Does this primary job apply equally to users on variable or irregular incomes, where payday is less predictable?
- Does the emotional job shift for users who are managing debt versus users who are building savings?
- Is there a secondary job — habit tracking, partner transparency, saving for a specific goal — that serves a distinct segment?
