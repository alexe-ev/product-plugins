# Formulate Experiment Hypothesis - Transcript

## Input Analysis

**Product Context:**
- Used electronics marketplace (mobile web, Android focus)
- Current conversion rate: 3.2% (product card → purchase)
- Target segment: Android smartphone buyers

**Stated Goal:**
Raise conversion to 5% by implementing two changes:
1. Add seller video reviews to product card
2. Reduce checkout from 4 steps to 2 steps

## Reasoning Steps

### Step 1: Context Level Assessment
This is **rich-context input** because it includes:
- Clear product area (mobile e-commerce conversion funnel)
- Specific target segment (Android smartphone buyers)
- Current baseline metric (3.2% conversion)
- Specific interventions to test
- Quantified business goal (5% conversion target)

### Step 2: Identify Core Problem
The core problem is **low conversion on mobile web**. The two proposed interventions attempt to address different friction points:
- **Video reviews**: Addresses trust/confidence at the product card stage (pre-checkout)
- **Checkout simplification**: Addresses friction in the checkout flow (post-purchase-intent stage)

### Step 3: Distinguish Multiple Changes
**Critical decision from skill instruction #8:** "If multiple changes are mixed together, split them into separate hypotheses."

These are two distinct product changes:
- Different funnel stages (awareness/evaluation vs. checkout)
- Different mechanisms of action (social proof/trust vs. friction reduction)
- Different user experience elements
- Different expected impact vectors

**Result:** Will produce **two separate hypotheses** rather than one combined hypothesis.

### Step 4: Extract Intervention #1 - Video Reviews
- **Change:** Add seller video reviews to product card
- **Stage:** Product evaluation (pre-checkout)
- **Mechanism:** Increases trust/confidence via social proof
- **Target segment:** Android smartphone buyers
- **Primary metric:** Conversion rate (product card → purchase)
- **Expected direction:** Increase

**Causal reasoning:** Seller video reviews provide:
- Richer, more trustworthy content than static text/photos
- Demonstration of product condition/functionality for used electronics
- Reduced perceived risk for used goods purchases
- Visual proof of seller credibility

### Step 5: Extract Intervention #2 - Checkout Simplification
- **Change:** Reduce checkout steps from 4 to 2
- **Stage:** Purchase completion (checkout flow)
- **Mechanism:** Reduces friction and abandonment
- **Target segment:** Android smartphone buyers
- **Primary metric:** Conversion rate (cart → purchase confirmation)
- **Expected direction:** Increase

**Causal reasoning:** Fewer checkout steps:
- Reduce cognitive load on mobile screens
- Decrease form abandonment from step-to-step drop-off
- Speed up purchase completion
- Particularly impactful on mobile web where friction compounds

### Step 6: Handle User's Quantified Expectation
User states target is 5% conversion (up from 3.2%).
- Current uplift target: **+1.8 percentage points** or **+56% relative lift**
- From the skill: "Do not force numerical uplift assumptions at this stage unless the user already has them."
- User HAS provided this, so it's appropriate to include as **expected impact hypothesis**
- However, with two separate changes, the impact distribution is unclear (which change drives how much?)

### Step 7: Assess Missing Information
For a properly executed test:
- Interaction between the two changes (are they tested simultaneously or separately?)
- Segment breakdown within Android users (different phones, price ranges, purchase histories?)
- Definition of "conversion" (was it clearly defined in product tracking?)
- Minimum detectable effect / statistical power requirements
- Whether video reviews are pre-recorded or must be created (production constraint)
- Current video availability from sellers

### Step 8: Confidence Level Assessment
- **Inputs present:** product area, segment, baseline metric, specific interventions, business goal
- **Inputs missing:** clarification on testing approach (simultaneous vs. sequential), guardrails, exact mechanism definitions
- **Assessment:** **Context-informed** - sufficient data to formulate testable hypotheses, but sequential testing would be needed to attribute impact to each change
